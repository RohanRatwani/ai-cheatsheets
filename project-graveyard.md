# The Project Graveyard

*41 side projects. 3 of them work. Here is the honest list of what died and why.
From @theprocrastihacker*

Most "what I learned" posts are written by people showing you their one success. This is the other
38. I went through every folder on my drive and wrote down what actually happened.

---

## The count

| | |
|---|---|
| Total projects | 41 |
| Still alive and used by anyone | 3 |
| Never shipped at all | most of them |
| Built twice because I forgot I already tried | 2 ideas |

---

## Cause of death #1: I built the same idea twice

I have two resume tailoring tools. Different names, different stacks, built about a year apart. I did
not remember building the first one.

I also have two coding tutor apps. Same story.

And five wedding planning apps. Five. A mobile one, a web one, a marketing tool for the web one, and
two earlier attempts I had already abandoned.

**What I actually learned:** the ideas you keep coming back to are not signals that the idea is good.
They are signals that you like thinking about that idea. Those are different things. Now I keep one
file listing every project I have ever started, and I read it before starting anything.

## Cause of death #2: the idea was too big to finish

The pattern is embarrassing once you see it. My dead projects are almost all *more ambitious* than my
live ones.

A voice agent that answers restaurant phones. A wearable pendant that displays your NFT over
Bluetooth. A multi agent system that audits entire websites. A marketplace for local services.

Every one of those needs several things to work at once before anyone can use any of it. So nobody
ever used any of it.

**What I actually learned:** a big idea has to be right about five things. A small idea has to be
right about one. You do not have five guesses in you on a weekend.

## Cause of death #3: I finished it and told nobody

This one hurts the most, because the work was done.

I have a Chrome extension that writes LinkedIn messages. A VS Code extension that improves your
prompts. A CLI that generates specs for AI agents, fully built and tested, that I never published to
npm. I forgot it was finished.

**What I actually learned:** shipping is not the last step, it is the halfway point. Now I do not
count something as done until someone who is not me has used it.

## Cause of death #4: the folder is empty

Two of my projects are folders containing only more empty folders. I made the directory structure,
felt productive, and never wrote a line.

**What I actually learned:** setup is not progress. If your first hour produced no working thing at
all, that is a warning.

---

## The three that lived

Here is what is actually alive right now.

1. **An Android app that helps you delete photos.** Swipe left to bin, swipe right to keep. It is on
   the Play Store. Real strangers use it.
2. **A command line tool for managing secrets.** You can `pip install` it today. It does one thing.
3. **An Instagram account that runs itself.** A scheduled job renders a quote onto a template and
   posts it, every day. It has not needed me in months.

## What all three have in common

I looked for the pattern and it is boring, which is probably why it works.

- **Every one is small.** You can describe each in a single sentence with no "and".
- **Every one does exactly one thing.** No settings pages, no accounts, no dashboards.
- **Every one shipped.** Not "was finished". Shipped, to a place where a stranger could find it.
- **None of them were my best idea.** Genuinely. The dead ones were more interesting.

---

## The checklist I use now

Before I start anything, I answer these. If I cannot, I do not start.

- [ ] Can I describe it in one sentence with no "and" in it?
- [ ] Have I already built this? (Check the list. I have been wrong about this twice.)
- [ ] What is the smallest version a stranger could actually use?
- [ ] Where will it live when it is done, and can I put it there this week?
- [ ] If I get bored in ten days, is there something usable left behind?

That last one is the real filter. Most of my 38 dead projects would have survived as something
smaller. I just never let them be smaller.

---

## The uncomfortable part

I do not think the 38 were wasted. I learned the stack on those. But I told myself for years that I
was "building", when what I was actually doing was *starting*. Those feel identical from the inside
and they are not the same thing at all.

If you have a graveyard too, you are not behind. You just need to finish one small thing and put it
somewhere a stranger can find it.
